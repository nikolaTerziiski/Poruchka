## 0. The short version

You are not lost. The core loop is genuinely built and it works end-to-end — plan → materialize → nudge → escalate → confirm → receive, across web, API, and Telegram. That is the hard part and it is done. The codebase is disciplined in the places where sloppiness would actually hurt you.

But there is **one structural problem that outranks everything else in this document**, and it explains the feeling of being lost better than any bug does:

### 🔴 The product cannot be used from the phone it's designed for

✅ **VERIFIED.** Your landing page promises "**No app to open**" ([page.tsx:43](apps/web/app/page.tsx)). The product cannot deliver that.

The Telegram bot handles exactly four callbacks — copy, submit, snooze, skip. **There is no way to change a quantity from Telegram.** And `submitOrder()` hard-blocks when no line has a quantity ([telegram.bot.service.ts:128](apps/api/src/channels/telegram/telegram.bot.service.ts)), replying *"add quantities in the app."*

Now trace the very first reminder a new restaurant ever receives. `defaultQuantity` is `nullable().optional()` ([schemas.ts:76](packages/shared/src/schemas.ts)) and the plan form submits `null` for any blank field. There's no prior order to prefill from. So the first reminder arrives with **no quantities**, the staffer taps "Изпратена" — and gets told to go find a laptop.

Even once history exists, the loop only supports *"send exactly what we sent last time, yes or no."* Real ordering is *"we're low on pork, make it 30 not 24."* The moment reality diverges from the plan — which is every week — the staffer taps nothing, opens Viber, and types the order by hand. Do that twice and Poruchka is optional. The reminder becomes noise, the status board becomes a lie, and the owner stops trusting the dashboard.

**This is the single biggest reason this gets abandoned in week 2**, and it's why the depth elsewhere feels unsatisfying: you've built a receiving-and-reconciliation subsystem on top of a loop that can't yet place an order.

### The rest, in priority order

1. **Delete or gate the `/dev` endpoints.** Unauthenticated account takeover. One line of config.
2. **Fix the "tap Done, get nagged anyway" race.** The most trust-destroying *bug* in the product.
3. **Decide honestly whether RLS is real.** The schema comment claims a security control that does not exist.
4. **Fix the `/terms` and `/privacy` 404s.** ✅ Verified: registration has a *mandatory* checkbox agreeing to two documents that don't exist ([register/page.tsx:122](apps/web/app/register/page.tsx)). That's a legal exposure, not a broken link.

## 1. What's actually built (inventory)

**API (NestJS 11)** — full REST surface, all tenant-scoped behind `SupabaseAuthGuard`:
- `/me`, `/me/settings` — tenant settings (timezone, quiet hours, nudge interval, max nudges, language)
- `/suppliers`, `/items` — CRUD
- `/team` — members + Telegram link/unlink
- `/order-rules` — recurring order plans with grouped lines
- `/order-runs` — the full lifecycle: draft edit → submit → supplier-message copy → snooze → skip → confirm → receive (with per-line unit price, received qty, exception types)
- `/reminders` — calendar read-model
- `/health`

**Scheduler** — `@Cron(EVERY_MINUTE)`: materializes today's runs, dispatches nudges, honors quiet hours, re-nudges at an interval, escalates at `maxNudges` to the escalation user or a fallback OWNER.

**Telegram bot (grammY, long polling)** — deep-link account linking, inline buttons for Sent / Copy supplier text / Snooze / Skip, with an authorization check on every action.

**Web (Next.js)** — 7 real pages, all wired to live endpoints, no mock data left: dashboard, orders, schedules, items, suppliers, team, settings. Full BG/EN i18n.

**Shared** — zod schemas + recurrence logic.

**Not present:** CI (no `.github/workflows`), meaningful test coverage (2 spec files), history/reporting page, `/profile` route (linked from the sidebar → 404).

## 2. What is genuinely good

This is not filler. These are real and you should not second-guess them.

- **Tenant scoping is disciplined and consistent.** Every mutating path across items, suppliers, team, order-rules, and order-runs goes through `ensureOwned` / `getOwned` using `findFirst({ where: { id, tenantId } })` before touching a row. I traced all of them. **There is no IDOR in the authenticated REST API.** For a solo build, that consistency is the single best signal in this repo.
- **The Telegram surface is safer than most.** Long polling, not a webhook, so there is no forgeable HTTP endpoint. `authorizedRun` (telegram.bot.service.ts:251) scopes by `tenantId` + `chatUserId` and checks assignee / escalation user / OWNER before acting. Link codes are single-use with 48–64 bits of entropy.
- **`OrderRunLine` snapshots are the right call.** Historical orders stay truthful when the catalog or plan changes later. Decimal for money/quantity, not float. Correct instinct.
- **Idempotent run creation.** `@@unique([orderRuleId, dueDate])` + upsert + `skipDuplicates` means two clients racing to materialize the same week cannot create duplicate orders.
- **Quantity carry-forward** (order-runs.service.ts:59) — new runs inherit last cycle's quantities. Small touch, real operational value.
- **JWT algorithm is hard-pinned to ES256** (supabase-jwt.ts:41), so classic alg-confusion forgery is not possible.
- **The migration churn resolved cleanly.** The reconcile migration properly migrated data out of the old `schedules`/`reminder_instances` tables before dropping them.

## 3. Security findings

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Unauthenticated `/dev` endpoints ship to production
✅ **VERIFIED** — [dev.controller.ts](apps/api/src/dev/dev.controller.ts), [app.module.ts:17](apps/api/src/app.module.ts)

`DevController` has **no `@UseGuards`**, and `DevModule` is imported unconditionally with no `NODE_ENV` gate.

- `POST /dev/link/:userId` writes a fresh `linkCode` onto **any** user row, with no auth and no tenant filter, and returns the code + `t.me` deep link in the response body.
- `POST /dev/test-reminder/:userId` sends Telegram messages to any linked user.

**Exploit:** an unauthenticated attacker with any user UUID calls `/dev/link/:victimId`, gets the deep link, opens it in their own Telegram. The `/start` handler (telegram.bot.service.ts:67) binds the **attacker's** chat to the victim's account. `authorizedRun` then treats them as the victim — they receive that person's order reminders and can submit/skip/snooze orders as them. **No tenant filter means this works across every tenant.**

**Fix:** gate the import — `...(process.env.ENABLE_DEV_ENDPOINTS === "true" ? [DevModule] : [])` — or delete the module. It's throwaway pilot scaffolding; it has served its purpose.

### 🟠 HIGH — RLS is claimed but does not exist
✅ **VERIFIED** — [migration.sql:176](apps/api/prisma/migrations/20260713110000_reconcile_order_model/migration.sql), [schema.prisma:2](apps/api/prisma/schema.prisma)

`schema.prisma` line 2 says *"RLS policies are added in a separate SQL migration."* They are not. Reality:
- `ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` on 4 tables (`order_rules`, `order_rule_lines`, `order_runs`, `order_run_lines`).
- **Zero `CREATE POLICY` statements anywhere in the repo.** No `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`.
- `tenants`, `users`, `suppliers`, `items`, `message_logs` never enable RLS at all.
- The app connects as the table-owner role, which **bypasses RLS** regardless when no `FORCE` is set.
- No `SET LOCAL` / `current_setting` tenant GUC is ever issued.

So RLS is inert. Your entire tenant boundary is the hand-written `where: { tenantId }` in each controller. Today those are all correct — but there is **no database backstop**, and the comment creates false confidence during exactly the review where you'd want the truth.

**Fix — pick one and be honest about it:**
- **(a) Make it real:** enable + `FORCE` RLS on every tenant table, add `USING ("tenantId" = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid)` policies, connect as a non-owner role, and set the GUC per transaction in `PrismaService`.
- **(b) Drop the claim:** remove the `ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` lines and the schema comment, and add a test asserting every model query is tenant-filtered.

For a single-restaurant pilot, (b) is the honest and correct choice. Do (a) before you onboard a second paying restaurant.

### 🟠 HIGH — No role enforcement; any member can escalate to OWNER
✅ **VERIFIED** — [team.controller.ts:25](apps/api/src/features/team.controller.ts)

The `OWNER`/`MANAGER`/`STAFF` enum is never checked anywhere in the API. Any authenticated member can create `OWNER` users, delete other members, change tenant settings, and rewrite order plans. Most seriously, any member can call `POST /team/:id/telegram-link` for **another** member, receive their deep link, and bind their own Telegram to that account.

This is currently latent — the web only ever creates one OWNER per tenant on first login, so there are no low-privilege web users yet. It becomes live the moment you add staff logins.

**Fix:** a `@Roles()` decorator + guard on team management, settings, and destructive endpoints. Forbid non-OWNERs from assigning the OWNER role.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Group-chat linking grants shared identity
⚠️ *reported* — [telegram.bot.service.ts:54](apps/api/src/channels/telegram/telegram.bot.service.ts)

Linking stores `ctx.chat?.id ?? ctx.from?.id`. In a private chat these are the same, but if someone runs `/start <code>` in a **group** the bot was added to, `chatUserId` becomes the group id — and then every group member's button taps resolve to that one linked user.

**Fix:** reject linking unless `ctx.chat.type === "private"`, and resolve the actor from `ctx.from.id` (the individual presser) rather than `ctx.chat.id`.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — JWT skips `aud` / `iss`, accepts tokens with no `exp`
✅ **VERIFIED** — [supabase-jwt.ts:59](apps/api/src/auth/supabase-jwt.ts)

Signature and expiry are checked, but `exp` is only enforced *if present* (`if (payload.exp && ...)`) — a token without an `exp` claim never expires. `aud` and `iss` are never validated.

**Fix:** require `exp`, assert `aud === "authenticated"` and `iss === "${SUPABASE_URL}/auth/v1"`.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — JWKS refetch on unknown `kid` is a DoS amplifier
⚠️ *reported* — [supabase-jwt.ts:45](apps/api/src/auth/supabase-jwt.ts)

Any request with an unrecognized `kid` triggers a forced outbound HTTPS fetch to Supabase's JWKS endpoint, inside the request path, before rejecting. No negative cache, no rate limit, no valid signature required to reach it. Cheap unauthenticated amplification.

**Fix:** TTL + minimum refetch interval, negative-cache unknown kids, add `@nestjs/throttler`.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Unbounded string fields
⚠️ *reported* — [schemas.ts:42](packages/shared/src/schemas.ts)

`createSupplierSchema`, `createItemSchema`, `createUserSchema` use `z.string().min(1)` with no `.max()` — unlike the order-rule schemas, which correctly bound their inputs. Multi-megabyte names/notes can be stored.

### 🟢 LOW — Hardening gaps
✅ **VERIFIED** — [main.ts:10](apps/api/src/main.ts)

`app.enableCors({ origin: true })` reflects any origin (the comment already admits this needs fixing). No helmet, no rate limiting, no global validation pipe. Impact is bounded because auth is a Bearer token rather than a cookie, so classic CSRF doesn't apply — but lock it down before launch.

Also: link codes never expire, and the guard's doc comment still describes HS256/`SUPABASE_JWT_SECRET` verification that the code no longer does (the secret is dead config in `.env.example`). Fix the comment before someone "restores" it and reintroduces forgery risk.

### 🟢 Web token storage
Supabase stores the session in `localStorage` by default ([api.ts](apps/web/lib/api.ts) reads it via `getSession()`). Standard for Supabase SPAs, but it means any XSS is a full token theft. Worth knowing; not worth re-architecting now.

## 4. Correctness bugs — the reminder engine

These matter more than the security findings for a family-only pilot, because they break the product's core promise.

### 🔴 The "I already marked it Done" bug — trust-destroying
✅ **VERIFIED** — [scheduler.service.ts:100-109](apps/api/src/scheduler/scheduler.service.ts)

`dispatch()` reads due runs (line 44), sends the Telegram message (line 75), then **unconditionally writes** `status`, `nextNudgeAt`, `sentCount`, and `postponedUntil` (line 100).

If the user taps **Sent** during that send window, the bot sets `status = SUBMITTED, nextNudgeAt = null` — and then dispatch's trailing update **overwrites it back to `PENDING` with a fresh `nextNudgeAt`**. The completed order is resurrected and keeps nagging. The same clobber silently discards a **Snooze** (`postponedUntil: null` on line 105).

There is no atomic claim: `sentCount` is a read-then-write (`run.sentCount + 1`), the only concurrency guard is a per-process in-memory `this.running` flag, and the message is sent *before* the write — so a crash mid-dispatch re-sends the identical reminder.

Nothing erodes trust in a reminder app faster than nagging someone about a task they already completed. **Fix this first among the correctness bugs.**

**Fix:** claim the row atomically before sending —
```
UPDATE order_runs SET "nextNudgeAt" = <future>, "sentCount" = "sentCount" + 1
WHERE id = ? AND status = 'PENDING' AND "nextNudgeAt" <= now() RETURNING *
```
— send only if a row came back, and never write `status` unconditionally after a send.

### 🟠 HIGH — Unlinked assignee is nudged forever and never escalates
✅ **VERIFIED** — [scheduler.service.ts:65-72](apps/api/src/scheduler/scheduler.service.ts)

If the assigned user has no `chatUserId`, dispatch logs a warning, pushes `nextNudgeAt` forward, and `continue`s — **without incrementing `sentCount`**. Since escalation triggers on `sentCount >= maxNudges` (line 99), it is never reached.

A new staff member who hasn't linked Telegram yet — a very common real-world state — means that order silently retries hourly forever, with **nobody ever notified**. The order misses its supplier cutoff and no alarm fires. This is a direct failure of the core promise.

**Fix:** treat an unlinked assignee as a delivery failure that counts toward escalation, or escalate to the owner immediately.

### 🟠 HIGH — Downtime permanently drops reminders
✅ **VERIFIED** — [order-runs.service.ts:11-19](apps/api/src/features/order-runs.service.ts)

`materializeToday()` materializes only `[today, today]`. If the process is down or asleep across a day, **no `OrderRun` is ever created for that day** — the reminders aren't delayed, they're gone. On a free/sleeping host tier this is not hypothetical.

**Fix:** backfill a small window (yesterday..today) on startup and each tick, and suppress or clearly mark reminders whose `dueAt` is already well past rather than firing them as fresh.

### 🟠 HIGH — Snooze resets escalation; postpone is uncapped
✅ **VERIFIED** — [order-runs.controller.ts:194](apps/api/src/features/order-runs.controller.ts), telegram.bot.service.ts:194

Both snooze paths do `sentCount: run.status === "ESCALATED" ? 0 : run.sentCount`, and both accept `ESCALATED` runs. So snoozing an escalated order flips it back to `PENDING` with the counter zeroed — it will escalate again, and can be snoozed again, forever. `postponedCount` is incremented but **never read anywhere**, so there is no cap.

The escalation cap isn't sticky: an order can be postponed past its supplier cutoff indefinitely without ever staying escalated.

**Fix:** don't reset `sentCount` when snoozing an escalated run (or forbid it), and enforce a max `postponedCount` / hard deadline at `cutoffTime`.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — `quietHoursStart === quietHoursEnd` disables quiet hours entirely
✅ **VERIFIED** — [scheduler.service.ts:161](apps/api/src/scheduler/scheduler.service.ts)

`if (start === end) return false`. A tenant that sets both equal — intending "quiet all day", or just mis-saving `0/0` — gets **no** suppression and nudges at 3am. Nothing validates against it.

### 🟠 HIGH — `GET` endpoints write to the database, with an N+1 pattern
✅ **VERIFIED** — [order-runs.controller.ts:53](apps/api/src/features/order-runs.controller.ts), [order-runs.service.ts:37-91](apps/api/src/features/order-runs.service.ts)

Both `GET /order-runs` and `GET /reminders` call `materializeRange()`, which loops day × rule and issues a **separate `findFirst` plus a separate `$transaction`** per pair. For the 63-day cap that's up to 63×N sequential round-trips and 63×N write transactions inside a plain GET.

A read-only calendar view mutates data on every page load — so a browser retry or proxy prefetch silently creates rows, and a wide date range is a cheap way to hammer your own database.

**Fix:** move materialization to the scheduler only (it already runs every minute), or batch it into one `createMany` with `skipDuplicates`.

### 🟡 Editing a plan doesn't reach already-materialized future runs
⚠️ *reported* — [order-runs.service.ts:62](apps/api/src/features/order-runs.service.ts)

`upsert` uses `update: {}` and lines use `skipDuplicates`, so an existing run is never reconciled against a changed plan. If anyone viewed a calendar week spanning a future date, that run is frozen with the **old** items and quantities. Whether your edit takes effect depends on whether someone happened to open the calendar first — confusing and hard to reproduce.

## 5. Data model & API design

- 🟡 **`OrderRule` → `OrderRun` is `onDelete: Cascade`** (schema.prisma:198). Hard-deleting a plan would erase every historical order, price, and receipt under it. The API only soft-deletes today (`archivedAt`), so it's not currently reachable — but for a purchasing system used in supplier disputes, that FK is one manual `DELETE` away from destroying your cost history. **Change to `Restrict`.**
- 🟡 **Missing audit fields.** `submittedByUserId` is recorded, but `confirm()` and `receive()` store no actor. There is no `confirmedByUserId` / `receivedByUserId`, and `OrderRunLine` has no `createdAt`/`updatedAt`. When a supplier disputes "who logged that only 8kg arrived at this price?", the system cannot answer. Worse, a partial receipt sets `receivedAt: null`, **discarding the timestamp**.
- 🟡 **Hard deletes collide with `Restrict` FKs → raw 500s.** Deleting a supplier with items, an item ever ordered, or a member ever assigned throws an uncaught `P2003` and returns HTTP 500. The UI shows a delete button that just breaks. Catch `P2003` → 409, or soft-delete.
- 🟢 **Unbounded list endpoints** — `/order-rules`, `/items`, `/suppliers`, `/team` have no pagination.
- 🟢 **Decimals serialize as strings** while computed siblings (`previousUnitPrice`) are numbers, so the client gets mixed types for the same concept.
- 🟢 **Price history truncated at a global `take: 500`** (not per item), so rarely-ordered items silently lose their price-change indicator.
- 🟢 **Dead code:** `createScheduleSchema` and `reminderStatusSchema` in `packages/shared/src/schemas.ts` reference the deleted schedules model. Recurrence logic is **duplicated in three places** (`packages/shared`, `apps/api/src/reminders`, `apps/web/lib`) and the API copy differs from the shared one — a real drift risk, since web could preview a rule as due that the API materializes differently.
- 🟢 **`/reminders` collapses statuses.** SUBMITTED, CONFIRMED, PARTIALLY_RECEIVED, and RECEIVED all render as `confirmed`, so the dashboard calendar overstates progress.

## 7. UX gaps

- **The dashboard calendar explodes.** ✅ Verified: `reminders.controller.ts` emits **one row per order line** (`for (const line of run.lines) out.push(...)`), and the dashboard renders **one chip per row** (dashboard/page.tsx:227). The *counts* are correctly deduped by `orderRunId` (line 150), but the grid is not. Three suppliers × 10 items on a Wednesday = 30 chips crammed into a ~120px day cell. The calendar becomes unreadable exactly when the customer is succeeding. Render one chip per **order** ("Метро — 8 артикула").
- **Calendar chips aren't clickable, and there is no single-order route.** You can see a Metro order is pending and have no way to reach it — no detail page, no deep link. Every path to acting on an order is: dashboard → notice → navigate to Orders → scroll to find it.
- **The empty state is a trap.** Dashboard's "Създай график" CTA → `/schedules` → where the **"New order plan" button is disabled** pending a supplier, item, *and* team member. The first click a new user makes hits a wall, and the hint doesn't say which prerequisite is missing or link to it.
- **The owner shows up as an email address.** `SupabaseAuthGuard` provisions `name: payload.email ?? "Owner"`, so `/team` lists `jockigog@gmail.com` as a person and it flows into Telegram escalation copy. The dashboard papers over it with `r.assignee.split("@")[0].split(" ")[0]` (dashboard/page.tsx:109). Fix the cause — collect a name at registration (the form already asks for restaurant name) — and add a way to edit your own name, which currently doesn't exist anywhere.
- **Creating a plan can instantly spam a reminder.** `materializeToday()` runs every minute. Create a daily plan at 14:00 with a 09:00 reminder time → `dueAt` is already past → `nextNudgeAt` is past → it fires within 60 seconds. The user's first experience of Poruchka is an unexpected ping for an order they just configured.
- **Plan edits reach today's order inconsistently.** Because `upsert` uses `update: {}` and lines use `createMany({ skipDuplicates: true })`: **adding** an item *does* propagate to an existing run, but **removing** an item or **changing a quantity** does not. The user edits, refreshes, and sees a half-updated order with no explanation.
- **`ReceiveDialog` is unusable at the loading dock.** A 12-line Metro order renders 12 cards × 4 fields = ~48 inputs in one modal, with no save-as-you-go and no "all received as ordered" bulk action.
- **No way to test the pipeline.** After wiring supplier + item + team + Telegram + plan, there's no "send a test reminder" button in the admin. `DevController` has one — unauthenticated and not meant for users.
- **`/profile` 404s.** ✅ Verified: linked from the sidebar (layout.tsx:198) on every screen; no such route.
- **Delete buttons 500, and the copy lies.** Deleting a supplier says *"Items tied to it will be left without one"* — but `Item.supplier` is `onDelete: Restrict`, so it simply fails. There's no exception filter, and `lib/api.ts` surfaces the raw body, so the user sees a Prisma stack fragment in a red box.
- **Dashboard overstates progress** — everything past SUBMITTED renders as `confirmed`.
- **Quiet hours can be silently disabled** by setting start === end, with no validation or warning (see §4).
- **Route protection is client-side only** — no `middleware.ts`; pages briefly mount before redirecting. Not a security hole (the API is the real boundary), but it flashes.

## 8. The cut list — what's over-built for right now

Said plainly, because you asked for a critic: some of this is procrastination dressed as architecture.

- **Receiving + reconciliation** (per-line received quantities, unit prices, exception types, price-change deltas) is a *big* subsystem, and it's built before you've confirmed anyone reliably taps "Done" on a reminder. It presumes someone stands at the delivery door with a phone reconciling line items. In a family restaurant, they will not — not at first. **This is the most over-built area in the repo.**
- **Multi-tenancy** was a deliberate, defensible call (it's genuinely cheap to keep, expensive to retrofit) — but it's now generating real security surface (RLS theater, no role enforcement) for **one** restaurant. Keep the data model; stop paying for the security theater until tenant #2 exists.
- **i18n (BG/EN)** for a Bulgarian family restaurant is polish you didn't need yet.
- **The design system** (`components/ds/*`) is nice work for a 7-page internal admin.

None of this is wasted long-term. But it explains the "kinda lost" feeling: the product's *depth* has run well ahead of its *validation*. You've built for the restaurant that already loves this, not the one deciding whether to try it.

## 9. Business risks — be honest with yourself

### Reposition: sell the painkiller, not the vitamin

Be honest about what this is today: **a vitamin, except in one specific scenario where it's a painkiller.**

Nobody's restaurant is dying because they occasionally forget the tomatoes — they call the supplier or they 86 the dish. That's a vitamin, and "never miss an order" is a weak pitch that also implies the owner is forgetful, which he'll resent.

It becomes a genuine, expensive, panic-inducing painkiller in exactly one moment: **the person who knows the orders leaves, is sick, or goes on holiday.** Suddenly nobody knows what to order, from whom, by when. A WhatsApp group holds no memory. A paper list on the wall holds memory until someone replaces it.

So the real product is **institutional memory of the order** — *Metro, Wednesday, 24kg pork, call before 11:00* — not the ping. Reframe toward **"your ordering knowledge doesn't walk out the door."** That reframing also tells you which features matter: quantity editing (§0), coverage/delegation, and history — **not** push notifications, and not receiving reconciliation.

### The other risks

- **Telegram in Bulgaria is the elephant.** Viber dominates; Telegram penetration among 50-something restaurant staff is low. The pilot channel was chosen for engineering reasons (Viber bots went commercial-only in Feb 2024), not user reasons. You are asking a restaurant to **install a new messaging app to receive a reminder**, while your actual competitor is already open on every phone. Test whether your own staff will install Telegram *before* building anything else.
- **But note the asymmetry — there's an open door here.** Sending the order *to the supplier* via a `viber://chat?number=+359...` deep link needs **no Viber Bot API and no commercial approval**. That path is available today and sidesteps the whole gate. Splitting `Supplier.contact` (currently one freeform string) into `phone`/`email`/`viber` and rendering one-tap `viber://`, `tel:`, `mailto:` buttons is probably the **highest delight-per-effort feature in this entire document** — it turns a 6-step copy-paste-app-switch into two taps.
- **Who types the quantities?** Someone must decide *this week's* number, and that decision requires walking the walk-in — so the decider is in an apron, not at a laptop. The product currently assumes otherwise. Until §0 lands, every order either goes out with last week's numbers (wrong) or bypasses Poruchka entirely (fatal).
- **Willingness to pay.** A Bulgarian family restaurant will pay maybe 20–50 BGN/month for something that visibly prevents a crisis or saves money. They will pay **zero** for a reminder app. The money is in the owner's report — *"you spent X at Metro, prices rose 12%, two orders were missed."* Note that you can derive spend from the **sent** order (planned qty × a remembered price) rather than from receiving reconciliation — 80% of the insight for 5% of the data-entry burden.
- **Single restaurant vs SaaS tension.** The risk isn't the multi-tenant schema (already paid for). It's that **SaaS-shaped thinking is driving feature choices ahead of your one real user.** Receiving, price analytics, EN i18n, and a seven-state machine are all things a *SaaS* would need. Your one restaurant needs editable quantities and a working onboarding.
- **Your unexploited advantage:** you have a real restaurant with real staff and real suppliers. Almost nobody else building in this space does. Run the pilot for two weeks and count **how many orders actually got placed through Poruchka versus around it.** That single number tells you more than this entire audit.
